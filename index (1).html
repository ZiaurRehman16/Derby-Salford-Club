A joint forum on how engineers are taught, and what the profession is facing.

The Derby–Salford Forum on Engineering Education and Contemporary Issues brings staff, researchers, and postgraduates from both universities together to examine engineering pedagogy alongside the pressures reshaping the profession — accreditation, sustainability, automation, and who gets to become an engineer.

Why a forum between two engineering departments

Derby and Salford teach engineering to different cohorts, under different pressures, and rarely compare notes.

The forum grew out of informal exchanges between engineering education researchers at both universities, who kept finding the same problems on each other's desks: how to assess work fairly now that generative AI is part of every student's toolkit, how to fill an accreditation panel's expectations without hollowing out the teaching, how to widen participation in a discipline that still skews narrow.

Rather than solve these separately, we meet to compare approaches directly — a curriculum change piloted in Salford tested against Derby's cohort, a placement model from one institution examined by staff who've never used it.

Sessions are led by a short provocation — a paper, a policy change, a piece of internal data — followed by open discussion. We are not a publication venue; findings shared here are preliminary and off the record unless a presenter says otherwise.

Participation is not limited to engineering education specialists. Practising engineers, employers, and postgraduate researchers are welcome to bring a question from outside the classroom.
